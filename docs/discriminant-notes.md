# LDA/QDA node models: native probe findings

Working notes from black-box probes of the pinned GUIDE 46.3 binary (and 46.2 where noted), 2026-10-27, in preparation for classification LDA/QDA node models. Nothing here is implemented yet, and no captures are frozen; treat it as the starting point for that item in [the coverage checklist](coverage.md). Data were iris (`tests/fixtures/datasets/iris.csv`) and seeded synthetic Gaussian designs with 2 or 3 classes, 2 to 4 signal and 1 to 8 noise variables, rounded to four decimals before being written for GUIDE.

## Running GUIDE

The interactive writer asks "1 for basic tree, 2 for trees with higher accuracy" before the node model; only the second shows the model menu, and the answer is not written to the batch file. The node-model codes differ by version:

| Version | Codes |
|---|---|
| 46.2 | 1 simple, 2 nearest-neighbor, 3 kernel, 4 LDA, 5 QDA, 6 linear splits with constant nodes |
| 46.3 | 1 simple, 2 nearest-neighbor, 3 kernel, 4 LDA, 5 QDA, 6 bivariate QDA, 7 linear splits with constant nodes |

LDA is the menu default. With codes 4 to 6 there are no interaction or linear-split prompts. Defaults in this mode are 5 split levels and minimum node size 10, independent of n (checked for n = 40 to 3000). A no-pruning run with explicit depth and minimum node size is:

```
 1  (model fitting)    "o.out"    1  (one tree)    1  (classification)
 4  (LDA; 5 QDA, 6 bivariate QDA in 46.3)    3  (no pruning)    "data.dsc"
 1  (estimated priors; 3 then a file)    1  (unit costs)    1  (default split count)
 2  then depth    2  then minimum node size
 0  (no LaTeX)    2  "o.coef"    2  "o.fit"    1  (no R)    2  (no plot code)
```

Batch mode still validates its inputs: depth must exceed 1 and minimum node size cannot exceed n/2, and fixed-size pruning requires at least 3 leaves, so a single-node fit is only obtainable from data the model classifies perfectly. The `.coef` file lists, per node, each variable used with its node mean and both coordinate vectors. The `.fit` file gives each case's terminal node, its two coordinate values (raw `x·a`, not centered by the printed means) and the observed and predicted classes. Both are better acceptance evidence than the report.

## Established

- **Discriminant coordinates.** Coefficients are the generalized eigenvectors of the between-class scatter B against the total scatter T (unweighted sums of squares about the node means), normalized so `a'Ta = 1`. Recomputing them reproduces native's to 8 printed digits at every iris node.
- **How native computes them, and their signs.** The 46.3 binary keeps its symbol table, and lldb traces of its LAPACK calls show the path: `ldacont` takes `dgesvd` (JOBU = 'N', JOBVT = 'A') of T, rotates both matrices into its right singular vectors V, and calls `dsygvx` (ITYPE 1, all eigenpairs, lower triangle) on `(V'BV, V'TV)` with a positive ABSTOL; the coefficients are `V z`. A positive ABSTOL sends `dsyevx` to bisection and inverse iteration (`dstebz`, `dstein`), and `dstein` makes each eigenvector's largest component positive; that and the SVD's signs fix the coordinate signs. The bundled LAPACK is 3.10 or later (it has `la_xisnan` and `dpotrf2`). A port of the reference 3.12 routines reproduces the first coordinate, sign included, at all 1492 recorded synthetic nodes (1208 LDA and QDA nodes plus 284 from the first probes), and the second coordinate at every node with three or more classes. Because of that normalization, a coordinate's total variance is `1/(n - 1)`; a coordinate with no between-class separation has pooled variance `1/(n - g)` (the `0.100000` printed for a 12-case, 2-class node is this, not a floor).
- **Splits.** Every split is `D <= c` on the node's first coordinate, with `c` the midpoint of adjacent sorted coordinate values (507 of 507 synthetic splits). Rows with missing values are routed by their imputed coordinate value, not by a separate learned direction (29 of 29 checked rows).
- **Cut candidates.** The candidate left-child sizes are the fixed grid guide-rs already uses for Poisson and quantile trees, with `s` the minimum node size: every size when `m = n - 2s + 1 <= 102`, otherwise `s + floor(j (m - 1) / 101)` for `j = 0..101`, in both cases excluding the two end sizes `s` and `n - s`. That leaves at most 100 cuts (the report's "Maximum number of splits: 100"). All 272 native cuts in large nodes lie on the grid, against 73 expected by chance, and no child ever has exactly `s` cases.
- **Cut criterion.** Each candidate cut is scored by the total training misclassifications of the two children, each fitted as a complete leaf (its own variable selection, coordinates and LDA or QDA rule) but with the children's raw class proportions as priors rather than the corrected leaf priors. Ties go to the most balanced split, then the smaller left child. This selects native's cut in 279 of 279 synthetic LDA splits used to find it and 358 of 360 held-out LDA splits (4 classes, minimum node sizes 5 and 15). It explains the median cuts: they are the balanced choice among tied error counts. Because children reselect their variables, the cut depends on variables the parent does not use: adding a column that is insignificant at the root (p = 0.8) moves the root cut. Corrected priors (268 of 279 in the minimum-error set), equal priors (28), pooled variance denominators `n` or `n - 1` (at most 225 with corrected priors), a single QDA coordinate in two-class children, and first, last, Gini, entropy or middle tie-breaks all fit worse.
- **Variable subset.** Each node fits its own subset of the candidate columns: numeric variables, then 0-1 dummies of each categorical variable's sorted levels (C variables become B), then a `.NA` indicator for each numeric variable with missing values, each competing on its own (a node can use `sl.NA` without `sl`). A categorical variable without missing values leaves out its first level; one with missing values keeps every level, so missing is the reference, and has no `.NA` column. Dummies have no missing values in `ftests`. Variables are ranked by the node's one-way ANOVA F p-value, computed by `ftests` on each column's observed values only (classes without an observed value drop out of the test; traced F statistics match this at every child fit with all classes observed), while the fit itself uses node-mean imputation. A column with F = 0 or no defined F (constant in the node, or observed in one class only) has p = 1. With K candidate columns, J of them with p < 1, and m below `0.4/J`, the node keeps the top m if m >= 2; if m = 1 it keeps those below `0.8/(K - 2)`, and if m = 0 those below `0.8/(K - 1)`, in both cases at least two. So p = 1 columns count toward the relaxed thresholds but not the first; lldb traces of `ftests`, `lin_depend` and `dsygvx` reproduce the kept count at 25158 of 25158 fits in 30 synthetic missing-value trees with this rule, against 25075 when J = K. `find_ldagood` ranks the p-values with `dsort`, SLATEC's DSORT (Singleton's quicksort), which is not stable: equal p-values (two indicators with the same class pattern, or several p = 1 columns) come out in its order, and that order decides which tied column is kept. A port reproduces all 824 traced `dsort` calls of one tree. Controlled probes with exactly set p-values fixed each threshold to three digits (K = 3 to 10, n = 24 to 200, balanced and unbalanced classes), and the rule reproduces the subset at 1208 of 1208 synthetic nodes, 25 of 25 iris nodes and 773 of 773 held-out nodes. GUIDE's own chi-square ranking agrees with the subsets in only 66 of 160 roots.
- **Dropping dependent columns.** When three or more columns are kept, `find_ldagood` passes them to `lin_depend`, which runs Alan Miller's `lsq` module (AS 274): `startup` with an intercept, `includ` of each row (weight 1) in candidate order, `tolset(10 epsilon)` and `sing`, dropping the flagged columns. The traced input holds raw values with missing ones coded `-huge(1d0)`, so the Givens updates overflow and `sing` flags such columns as dependent, sometimes the most significant one (traced in `lda-tree-missing`: x0 dropped from 20 of 26 calls). With two kept columns there is no check.
- **Rank-deficient total scatter.** After `dgesvd`, `ldacont` counts the singular values of T above `max(m, n) * epsilon * s1`. At rank one it skips `dsygvx` and uses the first right singular vector itself (unit length) as the only coordinate; this happens when the two kept columns are dummies that coincide or sum to one in the node, and reproduces native's `-0.70710678` coefficients and leaf (`lda-leaf-dummies-rank-one`). What it does at a higher deficient rank is untraced; guide-rs solves the problem in the leading singular vectors. When `lsq` flags all but one of three or more kept columns, native keeps the two best-ranked columns (traced at 5 fits).
- **Categorical trees.** With these rules, 50 of 60 synthetic depth-4 trees with categorical features (with and without missing values) reproduce native's predictions, as do the two frozen categorical trees. The differences come from degenerate coordinates: when one dummy pattern separates the classes, the other coefficients are rounding noise (around 1e-16) that differs between native and guide-rs, and that noise orders rows tied on the coordinate, so the cut sends them to different sides.
- **Number of coordinates.** After `dsygvx`, `ldacont` sums the positive eigenvalues and adds the largest ones, at least two, until they exceed 95% of that sum; the count is capped at the number of classes less one and is at least two. Two-class problems use one coordinate. A node with three of four classes and eigenvalues 0.74, 0.30 and 0.13 uses three; one with 0.63, 0.12 and 0.01 uses two. Coordinates on which the node's classes do not separate add the same term to every class's score.
- **Missing values.** Missing numeric values are imputed by the node mean of the observed values; this reproduces native coefficients at every node, where root-mean or zero imputation fails.
- **LDA leaves.** A case is classified by Gaussian densities on the node's coordinates (above) treated as independent: class means, a pooled variance per coordinate with denominator `n - g`, and the node's class proportions as priors. This reproduces all 150 native iris predictions at depths 1, 2 and 5. The printed priors are corrected (below), but predictions follow the raw proportions: in a leaf with two of three classes, a row with posterior odds 1.001 under the corrected priors and 0.9995 under the raw ones is predicted as native does only with the raw ones.
- **QDA leaves (46.3 code 5).** As LDA but with a per-class variance per coordinate (denominator `n_c - 1`) and the `log variance` term, still with independent coordinates, and always two coordinates, even in a two-class node. Reproduces all 150 predictions at depths 1, 2 and 5.
- **Bivariate QDA leaves (code 6).** A full per-class 2×2 covariance. Printed correlations match the sample correlation except for classes with two cases (below), and with native's printed correlation the rule reproduces every prediction in nodes whose class covariances are nonsingular.
- **Leaf priors.** The printed prior is the node posterior `π_c n_c / N_c` (normalized; `π` the training priors), corrected as `(N p + π_c) / (N + 1)` with N the root size, exactly `Context::correct_zero_posteriors`. Classes absent from the node keep `π_c / (N + 1)`. Explicit priors change only these; coordinates and cuts are identical to the estimated-priors fit.
- **Stopping.** A node splits only if `n >= ceil(2.5 × min_node)` (exact for minimum sizes 3, 5, 8, 10, 15 and 20), and children have more than `min_node` cases. Nodes the leaf model classifies without error do not split (52 mixed-class zero-error leaves above the size threshold); single-class nodes print "All cases classified as".
- **46.2.** LDA trees, coefficients and predictions on iris are identical to 46.3. QDA differs: 46.2 writes `NA` for the second coordinate of two-class nodes, chooses a different root cut (-0.0875 against -0.0840) and grows a different tree. 46.2 is not a regression reference for QDA.
- **Not observed.** The "crimcoords switched in node" messages of manual section 5.2 (46.1) appeared in no 46.3 or 46.2 run.

## Linear splits with constant leaves (option 7)

46.3 option 7 (46.2 option 6) grows trees whose nodes select columns and compute discriminant coordinates exactly as LDA nodes do (iris root coefficients agree to all printed digits), but whose leaves are constant: class counts, zero-posterior-corrected posteriors and the predicted class, as in simple classification trees. The batch file has no split-count prompt, and the report says "Linear discriminant splits and constant node models". Defaults are depth 6 and minimum node size 2.

- **Cut criterion.** The cut minimizes the count-weighted Gini impurity of the two children (iris root: 100/50 rather than the 75/75 split that ties on misclassifications). Children fitted as LDA or constant leaves and scored by misclassifications reproduced none of 16 synthetic trees; Gini, with the candidates and tie rules below, reproduces all.
- **Candidates.** Every cut leaving at least the minimum node size s on each side, including children of exactly s rows (unlike LDA trees, whose grid excludes the end sizes), for nodes of any size; ties go to the most even split, then the smaller left child. Nodes split only with at least 2.5 s rows, as for LDA.
- **Tied leaves.** Leaves with tied class counts predict the class with the larger zero-posterior-corrected posterior (also in simple trees; see [validation](validation.md)).
- **Agreement.** 80 of 80 synthetic depth-4 trees (2 to 5 classes, 120 to 500 rows, minimum node sizes 2 to 20, with and without missing values) and the three frozen trees (`lda-splits-tree-*`, both versions) reproduce native's predictions.

## Open questions

- **The second coordinate in two-class nodes.** It is a null vector of B, and 46.3 QDA depends on it. With two selected variables it is unique up to sign and matches native in 319 of 319 leaves. With three or more, the LAPACK port matches native's vector in 2 of 164 nodes: the null space is degenerate, so the vector depends on rounding in `V'BV` and on `dstein`'s random starts, and the port does not yet reproduce GUIDE's arithmetic there. It decides QDA cuts as well as leaves: QDA cuts match native in 20 of 20 nodes where no candidate child depends on it (or on a one-case class), and in 226 of 265 otherwise. LDA predictions do not depend on it.
- **Missing values: remaining differences.** The differences once put down to rounding between rows whose selected columns are all missing came from the selection count above and from tie order: the cut search orders rows on the first coordinate with the same `dsort`, so rows with equal coordinates follow its order. With both, 128 of 135 synthetic missing-value trees (5% to 20% missing in two of four columns, 2 to 4 classes, 100 to 300 rows) match native, against 117 before. In the two remaining cases examined, the kept columns of every child fit match native, but the cut differs at nodes where the candidate children have one-case classes, so the leaf rule for such classes is the next suspect.
- **Degenerate classes.** For a class with two cases native prints correlations of ±0.50 or 1.0 where the sample value is ±1, and its predictions in such nodes follow its printed value. Whether this is a guard or numerical noise is unknown; it should be a documented guard in guide-rs rather than reproduced.
- **Weights, P variables and wide nodes.** On synthetic designs (6 seeds, both `lda` and `lda_splits`), guide-rs reproduces native's predictions with weights of 0, 0.5, 1 and 3 (12 of 12; zero weights drop the row and positive weights count as 1), with a periodic P variable that guide-rs excludes and native ignores (12 of 12), and with 14 numeric variables on 40 rows and minimum node size 3, so that nodes have more variables than rows (12 of 12).
- **Pruning.** Fixed-size pruning (`0` in the batch file, `fixed_leaves` in guide-rs) of depth-5 trees reproduces native's predictions in 22 of 24 cases. In both others the unpruned trees agree, and so do every node's misclassification count, but two weakest links tie: native prunes the child first and reaches the requested size, while guide-rs's documented parent-before-child order removes the parent's whole branch and undershoots it (7 leaves to 2 instead of 6). This is the general pruning-path tie order, not an LDA rule. Cross-validation folds come from different random streams, so CV-pruned trees are not compared; the manual's LDA fish example (CV pruning, with `sex` as dummies) gives a single leaf and identical predictions in both.
- **Not yet probed.** Ensembles (LDA has been off in GUIDE's forests since version 24; guide-rs rejects them).

## Implementation

guide-rs implements LDA trees with these rules (`node_model: {"kind": "lda"}`), computing coordinates with a port of the reference LAPACK path above (`src/lapack.rs`) and dropping dependent columns with a port of `lsq`. Frozen captures: 12 single-node fits and 10 depth-4 trees with minimum node size 10 and at most 121 rows, with both pinned versions, which agree. guide-rs reproduces every single-node coefficient (signs included), statistic and prediction, and all 10 trees exactly (leaf partitions and training predictions) with both versions. On fresh synthetic designs without missing values, all 165 depth-4 trees reproduce native's predictions: 2 to 5 classes, 60 to 500 rows and minimum node sizes 5 to 20, including nodes far larger than `2s + 101` rows. With missing values, 128 of 135 do (above). QDA and bivariate QDA are not implemented. The cut search refits two leaves for each of up to 100 candidates per node, so it is the expensive step.
